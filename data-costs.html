00 · The short versionOne screen, three answers

The dashboard needs three kinds of data: the conversation (posts, comments, videos), the record (news, election data, fact-checks), and the context (ads, search trends, surveys). Most of the cost is in the first one. Most of the credibility is in the second.

The three-sentence answer

What: post-level X data, a small IG/FB watchlist, YouTube comments, RSS/news, election/map data, and optional Telegram, Threads, Reddit, ads and trends.

Where: X via the official API only ($5/1k posts, licensed; resellers are unlicensed and not recommended — see §2b); Instagram via the official Business Discovery API plus a small Apify watchlist; YouTube and news free; Facebook only via watchlist or research access; WhatsApp nowhere.

Cost: roughly ₹27–32k/month for a lean demo, ₹55–70k/month for the default pilot, ₹1.3–1.6L/month for a campaign — plus ₹1–2.5L one-time for annotation and legal.

The number that changes everything

The only fully legal X route is the official API: $5 per 1,000 posts. 1.2 million posts/month is about $6,000, not the $180 a reseller quotes. The cheapest legal way to run is to read far fewer posts: use X's Counts endpoint for volume and share-of-voice, and read only a targeted sample (100k posts ≈ $500 ≈ ₹44k). Separately, X's pay-per-use licence is meant for prototypes and limited apps; a multi-client product needs X's written approval or an Enterprise plan. Details in §2b.

The minimum post-level fields

Every conversation record should land in one common shape. Without these fields, the dashboard cannot compute share of voice, sentiment, language mix or coordination.

Required

  • Platform + external post ID — dedupe key
  • Account + author hash — entity map, never a person label
  • Timestamp — trend, velocity, timezone
  • Text / caption / title — the corpus
  • Language — detected, with a confidence
  • Public metrics — likes, comments, views, reposts where exposed
  • URL — evidence link
  • Source + capture time — provenance and retention

Useful, not always available

  • Geo tag or profile location
  • Media type (image/video/reel)
  • Parent post / reply / quote
  • Mentions and hashtags
  • Comment text (owned accounts or vendor only)
  • Reach, impressions, saves (platform-only)
  • Demographics (platform-only)
The derived layer

Sentiment, stance, emotion, sarcasm, narratives, coordination and quality badges are not data sources. They are computed from the text by models plus a human-labelled gold set. Budget for one LLM call per post/cluster and ₹0.5–1.5 lakh for annotation.

The one row to argue about

X via reseller is 25–30x cheaper than official X, but it is unlicensed and can break or be cut off without notice. For a commercial product sold to political clients, the 2026 recommendation is to drop the reseller as the production route (D3 → default to official). The reseller can stay as a throw-away tool for internal UI testing only, never for client-facing numbers.

02b · X / TwitterEvery route to X data, and which are legal

Checked 7 Nov 2026 against docs.x.com and vendor pages. Prices are public list prices in USD; enterprise vendors do not publish rates.

Bottom line

Cheapest fully legal route: X's official pay-per-use API, at $0.005 per post read, with the Counts endpoint ($0.005 per request) for volume and a small targeted sample for content. Cheapest route that is safe to resell to clients: a licensed listening platform (Brandwatch, Meltwater, Talkwalker, Sprinklr) used as a data source, or an X Enterprise / written approval. There is no cheap route that is both legal and built for multi-client resale.

Does your brief fit X's rules?

QuestionAnswer
1 · Permitted under the ToS and Developer Agreement?Partly. Commercial use is allowed. Analysis of X content is allowed only "as explicitly approved by X" (II.A.1); client-facing analytics is not addressed, so confirm in writing. No sell, sublicense, redistribute or service-bureau use (III.A).
2 · Enterprise, approval or licence needed?Likely yes once you serve several paying clients. Pay-per-use covers limited end-users; X can require Enterprise after a usage review (III.J, III.L, III.M). Government end-user display needs Enterprise (XIV.C).
3 · Higher-risk parts(a) Protest and rally coverage: X lists protests, rallies and community organising as sensitive events it does not allow tracking. (b) Political clients plus stance labelling: inferring political affiliation or beliefs is prohibited. (c) Resale of raw posts. (d) Public display of posts: no iframe embedding; only IDs may be redistributed.
4 · FieldsNeeded: post ID, text (for processing), timestamp, language, public engagement counts, URL. Avoid: usernames or user IDs in the store (X allows aggregate analysis only when no identifiers are stored; hashed IDs are a grey area, so ask X), location beyond the post, bio text for inference, follower graphs.
5 · SafeguardsAggregate-only outputs; no user-level views; delete or modify within 24 hours of an X deletion; no model training on X data; log source, sample size and capture date on every number; no DMs, no automation or posting; no individual bot labels; retain raw text only as long as the licence allows; a written X approval on file; legal review under India's DPDP Act (rules notified 13 Nov 2025, main duties from 13 May 2027).
6 · What can be inferred honestlyCan: volume, share of voice, topic and narrative clusters, tone of public posts, timing and spikes, which public accounts amplify a theme. Cannot: election outcomes or vote share, who a person supports, religion, caste or other traits of individuals, whether a named person is a bot, or what the silent majority thinks. X users are not a representative sample of Indian voters.

The accuracy trap

The dashboard can show sentiment on day one. It cannot claim per-language accuracy until the gold set exists. The MVP rule is explicit: no per-language accuracy claim before the V8 pilot (week 7) and the gold set (CP-3). That is why annotation is a cost line, not a nice-to-have.

The honest reading

The expensive parts of “dashboard depth” are not the pages. They are the X volume, the annotation, and the analyst time. The free layers — news, YouTube, election data, GDELT, Wikipedia — give the dashboard most of its credibility at almost no data cost.

Bottom line

The dashboard is buildable with real data at ₹27–32k/month for a lean version and ₹55–70k/month for the default pilot. The expensive parts are X volume, annotation and analyst time — not the dashboard itself. The ₹ figures use reseller prices for X and are not licence-safe; official X adds roughly ₹40k–5L depending on posts read. The biggest risks are X's licence scope for resale, Telegram’s terms, and Facebook’s lack of a public API. All three are decisions to make explicitly, not gaps to hide.
